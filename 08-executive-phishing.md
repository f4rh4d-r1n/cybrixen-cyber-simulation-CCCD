# 🎣 Mission 8: Executive phishing

> **Score: 10 / 10** 🟢 · 📍 CEO office · 🛠️ The CEO's tablet and its mail app · 🏷️ Email security, Social engineering

## 🎯 The mission
The CEO asked me to go through the inbox on their tablet. Report the phishing, keep the genuine mail. Same task as Mission 1, but the inbox belongs to the highest-value target in the company. 👔

## 🧠 How I approached it
Executives get **targeted** phishing (often called whaling), so I paid extra attention to messages that try to exploit authority, money or access:

| 🚩 Suspicious pattern | Why it works on executives |
|----------------------|----------------------------|
| "Approve these bonus letters" | Looks like a routine approval, but pressures a quick click |
| "Your tablet will be locked tonight, renew the security profile" | Fake IT urgency to harvest credentials or install something |
| "You've been invited to join an advisory board" | Flattery that targets status |

| ✅ Genuine pattern | Why I trusted it |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Board pack for a scheduled meeting | Fits the normal business rhythm and asks for nothing unusual |
| Signed engagement letter | Expected business document |
| Travel itinerary | Routine and low-risk |

## ✅ Result
All 6 messages classified correctly. 🎉

## 📚 Lessons
- Executives are prime targets because their accounts open the most doors.
- The same checklist works at every level: **urgency, authority, flattery, unexpected action**.
- Practice from Mission 1 paid off: 10/10 on both inboxes.
